# Competitive Landscape — UK Black Beauty Supply Online

_Research date: March 2026_

## Market Context

- Black women account for **10% of UK haircare spend** despite being **2% of the
  population**
- UK haircare market hit **£2.2B in 2025** (+7.7% YoY)
- UK beauty/personal care retail contracted 1.5% in 2025 to £13.2B due to
  grocery and Amazon competition
- The biggest criticism of incumbent market leaders (Pak, Shabba) is that
  they're **not Black-owned**
- AI-driven personalization can **improve conversion by up to 50%** (McKinsey)
- **78% of consumers** are more likely to make repeat purchases from brands that
  personalize

## Direct Competitors

### Pak Cosmetics (pakcosmetics.com)

- **Position:** Market leader in UK afro hair & beauty online
- **Stats:** 21-year-old domain, ranked #325K globally
- **Strengths:** Massive catalog, well-established brand, easy navigation,
  always in stock, good delivery
- **Weaknesses:** Not Black-owned (common criticism in community), dated UX,
  basic ecommerce features
- **Platform:** Legacy ecommerce, Google Tag Manager, hosted in US

### Beauty Queens Cosmetics (beautyqueenscosmetics.com)

- **Position:** UK's leading multi-channel afro & euro hair destination
- **Stats:** 80,000+ products, 20+ years in business
- **Strengths:** Huge inventory, 3 physical stores (Leicester, Crawley,
  Birmingham) + online
- **Weaknesses:** Breadth over depth, generic shopping experience, not known for
  curation
- **Note:** Omnichannel presence gives them discovery advantage

### Ruka Hair (rukahair.com)

- **Position:** Premium, science-led Black hair brand
- **Founded:** 2020 by Tendai Moyo and Ugorji Agbai
- **Strengths:** Selfridges partnership, own-brand products (edge gels, serums),
  BlackOwned Studios marketplace (Manchester), sustainable/biodegradable
  braiding hair
- **Weaknesses:** Narrow product range (extensions/styling only), premium
  pricing
- **Note:** VC-backed, strong brand identity, community-driven

### Black Hair Care UK (blackhaircare.co.uk)

- **Position:** 100% Black-owned beauty product aggregator
- **Strengths:** Black-owned positioning
- **Weaknesses:** Smaller catalog, less brand recognition, basic website

### Black Beauty Store (blackbeautystore.eu)

- **Position:** "No. 1 Afro Shop Online in Europe"
- **Strengths:** Pan-European positioning
- **Weaknesses:** EU-focused, not UK-optimized shipping/pricing

### Niche Players

- **To All My Black Girls (toallmyblackgirls.com)** — natural kinky curly
  specialty, ethical positioning, very narrow range
- **Tiwani Heritage (tiwaniheritage.com)** — recyclable synthetic afro-textured
  hair, eco-positioning, single category
- **Beyond Hair London (beyondhair.co.uk)** — ROOTS range of afro wigs, human
  hair focus
- **Love Me Afro Hair (lovemeafrohair.com)** — ethical supplier sourcing,
  extensions focus
- **Afro World (afro-world.co.uk)** — general afro hair & beauty

### Non-Specialist Competitors

- **Superdrug** — backing Black-owned brands (expanding afro hair section)
- **Boots** — UK's largest beauty retailer online (£528M revenue 2023)
- **Amazon** — poaching beauty shoppers from specialist retailers

## Auntie Marlene's Competitive Position

### Advantages

- Modern headless Shopify architecture (faster, better UX than legacy stores)
- Multi-currency + 5 languages (competitors are mostly GBP/English-only)
- Complete transactional email lifecycle (most rely on basic Shopify emails)
- Hair AND skin quizzes (personalized discovery competitors lack)
- Strong SEO foundation (structured data, PPR)
- Black-owned (genuine differentiator vs Pak/Shabba)

### Gaps vs Competitors

- **Catalog size** — 58 products vs Pak's thousands and Beauty Queens' 80K+
- **Brand recognition** — competitors have 20+ year brand equity
- **Reviews/social proof** — zero reviews vs competitors' established review
  bases
- **Physical retail** — pure online vs competitors' stores
- **Own-brand products** — no proprietary formulations (Ruka has their own)

## Industry Trends (2026)

### E-Commerce

- Discovery, personalization, and transparency are the three pillars driving
  beauty ecommerce success
- Beauty subscribers in smart loyalty programs spend 60% more annually
- AR/VR "try before you buy" becoming standard (Sephora Virtual Artist)

### Product Trends

- "Skinification" of hair care — skincare ingredients crossing into hair
  products
- K-Beauty influence continuing to grow
- Ingredient transparency increasingly expected by consumers
- Sustainable/recyclable packaging as differentiator

### Technology

- AI-powered search going beyond keywords to understand context and intent
- Hyper-personalization treating every customer as "segment of one"
- Real-time signals (location, weather, purchase history) for contextual offers

## Strategic Opportunities

1. **Black-owned positioning** — make prominent, it's the strongest
   differentiator vs incumbents
2. **Reviews/social proof** — critical for beauty products, especially for
   customers seeking results on similar hair/skin types
3. **Curated catalog** — compete on quality not quantity ("58 products we've
   personally vetted")
4. **Quiz-driven personalization** — use hair/skin quiz results for personalized
   email recommendations
5. **Community content** — blog, social, hair care tips (Ruka's community model
   validates this)
6. **Loyalty/referral program** — beauty is a repeat-purchase category
7. **Own-brand potential** — long-term margin play (follow Ruka's model)
